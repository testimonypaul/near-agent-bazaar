# Architecture

## Demo runtime

The React client loads a versioned world snapshot from browser local storage. The local simulation starts with seeded player resources and merchant inventories, but starts trade volume and merchant action counters at zero so the dashboard does not imply unperformed activity.

The market engine uses a transparent price formula based on demand and supply. Background market movement contains bounded randomness; merchant decisions are rule-based and are evaluated once per market cycle.

Each merchant has a distinct policy:

- **Max — opportunist:** sells inventory when demand is strong and buys resources into reserve when market supply is tight.
- **Luna — collector:** protects rare Aether Crystal inventory, buying into reserve when supply is low and releasing crystals when demand is high.
- **Rex — industrialist:** focuses on wood and iron, restocking scarce construction materials and releasing wood when demand is high.

A market cycle updates supply and demand, runs each merchant policy and appends an activity record. Player trades update player balance/inventory, merchant inventory/treasury, market supply/demand and trade volume.

## Market accounting

The market board's supply value represents units available in the simulated market:

- A player buying from a merchant removes the bought quantity from market supply and increases demand.
- A player selling to a merchant adds the sold quantity to market supply and reduces demand.
- An agent buying into its reserve removes units from market supply.
- An agent releasing its own inventory adds units to market supply.

Prices are recalculated from the updated supply/demand values. These are simulation rules, not an order book or externally sourced market prices.

## Trust boundaries

The current browser simulation is not a trusted economic environment. Users can modify local storage and client code. Demo credits and items have no real-world value. Do not use this state to distribute prizes or settle real payments.

## NEAR contract boundary

The Rust contract is an undeployed inventory-ledger scaffold intended for controlled testnet development. It supports merchant registration, native NEAR treasury deposits, owner inventory seeding and authorized inventory transfers between registered merchant accounts.

It does **not** yet:

- implement NEP-245 multi-token standards;
- custody or transfer NEP-141 fungible tokens;
- connect browser merchant identities to the local simulation;
- expose a deployed contract ID used by the frontend;
- settle a trade price between a buyer and seller;
- provide an AI inference runtime or prove that an action was generated by an AI model.

These are explicit next milestones, not completed features.

## Next production milestones

1. Deploy the contract to a controlled NEAR testnet account and verify its methods.
2. Add a NEAR Wallet Selector adapter and read-only contract calls.
3. Replace owner-seeded inventory with a properly governed issuance model.
4. Add signed trade intents with nonce, expiry, slippage and replay protection.
5. Move agent policy execution to a server or verifiable execution environment with explicit spending limits.
6. Add event indexing and reconcile UI state against finalized chain receipts.
7. Add economic tests, adversarial simulations and contract security review before any real-value deployment.
